# ASH RIVER — Series Bible

**Genre:** survival thriller
**Format:** 12 × 7-minute episodes, assembling into an ~84-minute feature cut
**Logline:** One road out of a burning valley, eleven passengers, and a driver who has
made this exact drive before — the last time, three people did not come back.

---

## Premise

Ash River is a valley town with a single road out: Ridge Road, forty-one miles of
switchback to Carrow Gap. When the fire crosses the west ridge four hours ahead of
forecast, **Maren Vale** — school bus driver, and eleven years ago a wildland fire crew
boss — takes the last bus out with eleven passengers.

A voice on the radio guides her. The road ahead keeps closing. And the fire is behind
them, which is the wrong place for it to be.

---

## The premise test

The six questions from `doctrine/genre_survival.yaml`. A concept below 5/6 is killed;
this one answers all six, and the last two are the ones that decide whether a series has
a spine.

**1. Engine.** A vehicle moving through a closing fire corridor generates its own next
problem without an author: road blocked, fuel gone, wind shifted, smoke thickened,
someone hurt, someone panicking, a bridge gone. The situation never needs help.

**2. Confinement.** A bus is a room with a view of the thing that will kill you. Getting
off is not an option, and the road has no exits — the boundary is enforced by physics,
not by a rule anyone could argue with.

**3. Depleting quantity.** Three, in sequence:
- **Tier 1 — fuel.** A punctured tank. The gauge is on the dash, in frame in almost
  every driving shot. Exhausted by E04.
- **Tier 2 — the route.** The map of open roads, one closing per episode. Exhausted at
  E08, when they learn the last open road was never open.
- **Tier 3 — Maren's hands and her conscience.** Burned on the door lever in the first
  minute, worse every episode. By E12 she physically cannot do the thing she must do.

A fourth, secondary clock runs alongside: **Aunt Bri's oxygen tank** — a gauge that is
also, in a fire, a bomb.

**4. Internal enemy.** There are twelve people on a bus that boarded eleven.

**5. Engineerable.** Yes, and this is the season. The fire's ignition points form a line,
not a spread. The alternate route was gated three weeks ago. The valley is being cleared,
and the evacuation was routed by the office that lit it.

**6. Protagonist complicity.** Yes, and this is the series. Maren was crew boss on the
Coldwater burn eleven years ago. She called a crew into a slot canyon, the wind turned,
and she came out. Eight went in behind her; three did not come back. She left the valley,
came back quiet, and drives a school route now. **The reason she knows this road is that
she has already run it once, in the dark, alone.**

## The twist ladder (pre-committed — see `season-01/twist-ledger.yaml`)

**L4 — the season twist (E08). The premise was engineered.**
The fire was set. The gate at the north cut was installed three weeks before the season
started. Every obstacle the audience read as bad luck converts, in one beat, into intent
— and the "dispatch" that has been routing them is the office that did it.

**L5 — the series twist (E12). The protagonist is a cause.**
The voice on the radio is Ronan Coyle, son of one of the three who did not come back
from Coldwater. He has not been guiding Maren out. Every turn he gave her has taken her
further from Carrow Gap and closer to the same slot canyon, because he needs her to
stand in front of witnesses and make the identical choice — leave someone, and live.

The cruelty of it is that he is right about her. In E12 she makes the choice.

**These two are fixed before Episode 1 is written.** They cannot be revised later
without violating `forbid_retcon`, because the clue trail is planted from the first
ninety seconds of the series.

**L3 — arc twists.**
- **E03** — the radio is not dispatch. County comms went down forty minutes before the
  bus left. She has been taking directions from a private channel.
- **E06** — Ines, the calm one, surveyed the ignition line. She is not a saboteur: she
  filed the warning, and it was buried. She is on this bus because she thinks it is her
  fault too.
- **E09** — Theo's notebook carries Maren's old crew call sign, in a child's handwriting.
  He did not wander onto this bus. He was put on it.
- **E12** — the twelfth passenger is Coyle himself, and he has been on the bus since
  before the door opened.

## Cast

Twelve on the bus. Small enough to track, large enough to lose people.
Every one of them wants something incompatible with survival.

| | Character | Wants | Incompatible because |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Maren Vale**, 41 — driver, ex-crew boss | to bring everyone out | she will trade one for eleven, and knows it |
| 2 | **Theo Barrow**, 9 — draws, rarely speaks | to go back for something | it is behind them |
| 3 | **Ines Ruiz**, 36 — county surveyor | to be believed | being believed means admitting what she mapped |
| 4 | **Dell Prentice**, 58 — town mechanic | to be listened to | he is right at the wrong volume |
| 5 | **Brianne "Bri" Ott**, 63 — Theo's great-aunt, oxygen | to not be a burden | her tank is the loudest thing on the bus |
| 6 | **Sam Okwuosa**, 24 — line cook, filming | to record it | the phone is evidence someone wants |
| 7 | **Ruth Vane**, 71 — retired teacher | to make Maren say it out loud | saying it costs Maren the cabin |
| 8 | **Kit Marsh**, 17 — headphones, watchful | to be useful once | the useful thing is the dangerous thing |
| 9 | **Danny Prentice**, 31 — volunteer firefighter | to take the wheel | he is competent and wrong |
| 10 | **Nel Alvara**, 45 — nurse | to go back for the clinic | she does, in E02. She is the rules being established |

**Ronan Coyle** — a voice on the radio. Call sign *Dispatch 9*. There is no Dispatch 9.

## World rules

1. **The bus is the only safe place, and it is not safe.** Anyone outside it for more
   than ninety seconds does not come back in the same condition.
2. **The fire moves with the wind, and the wind turns at dusk.** Established in E01, paid
   in every episode after.
3. **Nobody is stupid.** Every fatal decision is defensible from that character's
   information. Where a character is wrong, the audience can see exactly why.
4. **Radios are one-way trust.** You can hear a voice. You cannot see who is holding it.
5. **The road only goes one way.** There is no turning a forty-foot bus on a switchback.

## The boundary shrinks, per episode

`the valley → Ridge Road → the corridor between two burns → the bus → the gap at Carrow`

E01 they have six ways this could go. E12 they have one, and it costs.

## Look and sound

- **Palette.** Desaturated to the point of grey — except the fire, which is the only
  saturated thing in any frame. Each principal carries one identifying colour so a cast
  of twelve stays legible in a smoke-filled bus.
- **Camera.** Handheld inside the bus, locked-off outside it. The audience learns which
  side of the boundary they are on before anyone explains there is one.
- **Lens.** Long, compressing the road so the fire always looks closer than it is. Wide
  only when someone is outside and exposed.
- **Sound.** The clock is audible before it is visible: the fuel-pump stutter, the tank
  regulator's tick, the radio's carrier hiss with no dispatch ident under it.
- **Standards.** Threat is shown, injury implied. The cut lands before impact; deaths land
  on the reaction shot. This is a craft choice before it is a compliance one — implication
  makes the audience's imagination do the work, which is the strongest tool available.

## What this show is actually about

A woman who counts people. She counts them at the door, she counts them at every stop,
and she starts every count at nine — which nobody notices for eleven episodes, because
the audience assumes she is picking up where she left off.

She is.
