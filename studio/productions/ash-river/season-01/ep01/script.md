# ASH RIVER

## Season 1, Episode 1 — "HEADCOUNT"

*Running time: 7:00. Beat ids in the right margin correspond to `beatsheet.yaml`.*

---

**BLACK.**

A sound before a picture: metal ticking as it heats.

A woman's voice, mid-count, close.

> **MAREN (V.O.)**
> — nine. Ten.

---

### 0:00 — SCENE 1: THE DOOR — EXT. ASH RIVER ELEMENTARY, 6:41 PM `b01`

**SMASH IN** on a hand closing around a bus door lever. The lever is the colour metal
goes before it stops being safe to touch.

The hand closes anyway. Holds. Pulls.

The door folds open and smoke comes in around the frame in a sheet, orange from below.

MAREN VALE, 41, work jacket the colour of a fire shirt that has been washed four hundred
times, does not look at her own hand.

> **MAREN**
> Move. Move, move —

Bodies come past her, low. She does not step aside for any of them.

---

### 0:14 `b02`

The lever swings.

It swings *before* her hand reaches it — someone inside has pulled it the rest of the way.

She does not notice. She is counting.

> **MAREN**
> Nine. Ten. Eleven.

JOSIE KWAN, 29, teacher, comes up behind the last of them with a clipboard, counting too,
under her breath, and audibly starting at one.

> **JOSIE**
> — six, seven, eight, nine, ten, eleven. Eleven. That's all of them.
>
> **MAREN**
> Eleven.

She says it like she is agreeing with a number she got to a different way.

She swings up into the seat. The door folds. Her palm leaves a mark on the wheel.

---

### 0:38 — SCENE 2: RIDGE ROAD — INT. BUS, MOVING `b03`

The bus pulls out. In the long mirror the town goes orange behind them, one street at a
time, like something being switched off.

THEO BARROW, 9, yellow raincoat, spiral notebook held two-handed against his chest, stands
in the aisle looking for a seat.

Maren does not look at him. She reaches back, flat-handed, and moves him into the seat
directly behind hers.

She does not explain it. He does not ask.

---

### 1:00 `b04`

JOSIE works the aisle against the motion of the bus, chart up against the window light.

One row on the chart is filled in a different pen.

She does not see it. She is looking at faces.

DELL PRENTICE, 58, red cap, one arm held against his body:

> **DELL**
> Turn that up.
>
> **DANNY PRENTICE**
> There's nothing on it, Dad.
>
> **DELL**
> Then turn it up so we can all hear there's nothing on it.

Behind them, Theo's notebook. The bottom corner of it is burned. Already. Neatly, the way
paper burns when nobody was in a hurry about it.

The first switchback. The smoke reaches them. Somebody starts coughing and does not stop.

---

### 1:30 — SCENE 3: THE VOICE — INT. BUS `b05`

GUS TRELL, 52, off-duty, thumbs his handheld.

Nothing. Not static — *nothing*. No carrier at all.

> **GUS**
> Repeater's down.

Behind him the bus set hisses — clean, live — and a voice comes out of it that is calmer
than anyone on this bus.

> **COYLE (RADIO)**
> Ash River bus, Dispatch nine. You're going to want the Coldwater turn. Not the highway.
>
> **MAREN**
> Coldwater adds nine miles.
>
> **COYLE (RADIO)**
> The highway adds forty-one, because the highway's gone.

A beat.

She takes the Coldwater turn.

---

### 2:00 `b06`

INES RUIZ, 36, wraps Maren's right hand one-handed while the bus moves, bracing against
the seat back.

The grade steepens. Maren's hand goes to the stick and will not close on it.

> **INES**
> Let me —
>
> **MAREN**
> I've got it.

She has not got it. She rides the gear and the engine complains and everyone hears it.

> **INES**
> What was it?
>
> **MAREN**
> Door lever.

Across the aisle, RUTH VANE, 71, is watching the wrapping. Not the fire. The hands.

She opens her mouth.

She closes it.

She opens it again, and closes it again, and looks out of the window.

---

### 2:25 — SCENE 4: THE GATE — EXT./INT. NORTH CUT `b07`

The bus stops.

A galvanised county gate stands across the north cut. Chained. New — the galvanising has
not gone dull yet.

> **MAREN**
> *(half to herself)*
> That wasn't there in June.

> **DANNY**
> Long way's still open. I ran it Tuesday.

He is thirty-one, he is a volunteer firefighter, and he is the most competent person on
this bus. He is completely certain.

> **MAREN**
> Nine miles.
>
> **DANNY**
> Nine miles we've got.

---

### 2:52 `b08`

KIT MARSH, 17, is out at the chain with a tyre iron before anyone tells him not to be.

**MAREN** (out the window): Ninety seconds.

Handheld inside the bus. Locked-off outside it — Kit small in the frame, the ridge behind
him doing something the audience can see and he cannot.

He comes back at eighty. Nothing to show for it.

He sits down, and pulls his sleeve over his forearm, and does not mention it.

On the dash, the fuel gauge is going down faster than the odometer is going up.

---

### 3:20 — SCENE 5: THE TANK — INT. BUS `b09`

A sound starts. A tick, then a tick, then a tick.

The camera finds the gauge on BRIANNE OTT's oxygen cylinder before Bri does. It is in the
red.

Bri, 63, lavender cardigan, does not look down. She has decided not to look down.

DELL, over his shoulder, to nobody:

> **DELL**
> Who's the fella in the back with the boots?

The regulator alarm goes off — a hard, flat, domestic sound, the sound of a smoke alarm in
a kitchen — and every head turns to Bri.

Nobody answers Dell.

Nobody ever answers Dell.

---

### 3:48 `b10`

DANNY on his knees in the aisle with the regulator.

> **DANNY**
> It's the seat, it's not the tank. It's the seat.

It takes him forty seconds. He gets it. The tick stops. Bri breathes and the whole bus
breathes with her, which is how you know they were all listening.

> **DANNY**
> That's an hour.
>
> **JOSIE**
> An hour's fine. An hour's Carrow.
>
> **DANNY**
> An hour's Carrow the long way. North cut's open, I checked it Tuesday.

Everyone hears him say Tuesday. Nobody hears it as a problem.

Maren looks at the gauge. Then at the mirror. Then she commits.

---

### 4:15 — SCENE 6: SAM'S PHONE — INT. BUS `b11`

SAM OKWUOSA, 24, has had his phone up since the school.

> **SAM**
> No — go back. Go back further.

He scrubs backwards. Three weeks. The ridge in daylight.

> **SAM**
> That's the gate. That's — there's no gate.

There is no gate.

He scrubs again. Same week. Evening. The ridge line at distance:

A row of small fires along it. Six of them. Evenly spaced.

---

### 4:40 `b12`

*No dialogue.*

The phone goes down the bus, hand to hand.

Eleven faces lit by a screen the size of a hand, and outside the windows the only other
light in the world.

Twenty seconds. Nobody says anything.

---

### 5:00 — SCENE 7: THE SECOND COUNT — INT. BUS `b13`

The north cut.

It is open.

Danny was right. Danny is quietly, enormously relieved to have been right.

Maren takes it — and the moment the bus is committed to it, Carrow Gap is behind them,
and the fuel they have is the fuel they finish on.

---

### 5:18 `b14`

Habit. She counts the cabin in the long mirror, the way she has counted every load of
children she has ever driven.

> **MAREN**
> *(under)*
> Nine. Ten. Eleven.

Beat.

> **MAREN**
> Twelve.

She stops. Her eyes come off the road, which they have not done once.

She counts again. Slowly. All the way down the mirror.

> **MAREN**
> Twelve.

Down the aisle, JOSIE is doing her own count, on her chart, in pen, the way she has done
it four times tonight.

> **JOSIE**
> *(to herself, satisfied)*
> Eleven.

Maren looks at the mirror.

Maren looks at Josie.

Maren says nothing at all, and puts both wrapped hands back on the wheel.

---

### 5:50 — SCENE 8: THE CORRIDOR — EXT./INT. BUS `b15`

Two ridges burning. A green corridor between them, untouched, and the road running down
the middle of it like something laid out for the purpose.

Long lens. The fire is always closer than it is.

Inside: Maren, driving. She checks the mirror four times in fifty seconds and does not
meet a single eye in it.

> **COYLE (RADIO)**
> You know this road.
>
> **MAREN**
> Everybody knows this road.

One beat. Two.

> **COYLE (RADIO)**
> Not like you do.

She does not answer that.

She should turn around and tell them there is one more person on this bus than there is
supposed to be. She knows she should. She has known for ninety seconds.

She keeps driving.

---

### 6:42 — SCENE 9: HEADCOUNT — INT. BUS `b16`

> **COYLE (RADIO)**
> Ash River bus, Dispatch nine. Headcount.

Maren's eyes go up to the mirror.

Twelve faces come back.

> **MAREN**
> Eleven.

Silence on the carrier.

One second. Which is one second too long.

> **COYLE (RADIO)**
> That's right. Eleven.

**FREEZE** — not on the road. On her face.

**CUT TO BLACK.**
