# Packaging

## Mandate
Win the impulse. Everything upstream is retention; this is the capture. The best episode
in the world has zero retention if nobody starts it.

## The mechanisms (pre-narrative attention)
1. **Curiosity gap in the title.** State an unresolved specific, not a genre.
   "ASH RIVER" is a name. *"One road out. Eleven passengers. One of them started it."*
   is an impulse.
2. **The 3-second contract.** The first frame poses a question answerable only by
   watching: motion, a face mid-reaction, a legible threat.
3. **Loss framing beats gain framing.** "They have 40 minutes of fuel" outperforms
   "they might escape". Threatened loss is a stronger driver than possible gain.
4. **Specific numbers.** Concrete quantities read as true, and start a countdown the
   viewer runs in their own head before pressing play.
5. **The unfinished sentence.** Copy that stops one word early inherits the Zeigarnik
   effect before a single frame plays.

## Method
Produce **at least 5 variants**, each scored 0–10 on: curiosity gap, specificity, loss
framing, 3-second legibility at thumbnail size, and honesty. Ship the highest total,
hold the rest for rotation.

## The honesty constraint
A package that promises something the episode does not deliver buys one view and costs
the series. The audience punishes the bait far more than they reward the hook, and the
punishment lands on the *next* release. **Score honesty last and treat it as a
multiplier, not an addend** — a dishonest variant scores zero regardless of its other
numbers.

## Failure modes
- Titles that describe the genre. Nobody clicks "survival thriller".
- Thumbnails legible only at full size. Design at 120px wide and check there.
- Reusing the same face at the same size every episode; the feed reads it as one item.
